Playback privacy policy

Last updated November 17, 2026

Playback is a small personal app for tracking music, shows and films. This page explains what happens to your information if you choose to sign in with Google to add friends. If you never sign in, nothing in this policy applies: your log stays in your own browser.

What Playback keeps if you sign in

  • Your Google account's stable id and the first name Google gives us (you can change the name friends see).
  • A friend code, and who you are friends with or have asked.
  • A short list of your album, show and film ratings: title, artist and your rating. Nothing else from your log is uploaded unless you turn on the next option.
  • Only if you turn on Keep my log on all my devices in Settings: a copy of your whole log (ratings, listening history, notes, collection entries, shows and films; not photos), stored as one compressed file that only you can read, so it can follow you to your other devices. Friends never see it. You can turn it off, delete the saved copy, or delete your account (which erases it) at any time.

Playback does not store your email address, your Google password, your contacts or your profile photo, and it does not use advertising or analytics trackers.

Who can see it

Only friends you have accepted can see your ratings, and you can turn that off at any time in Settings. Nobody else can see them. The person who runs Playback can see a list of accounts (name, join and last-seen dates, and counts of ratings and friends), but not what you rated. Your information is not sold or shared with anyone else.

Where it is stored

On Cloudflare (the Worker and database that run Playback's sync). Google sign-in is used only to confirm who you are.

Deleting it

Open Settings, Account, then Delete my account. That erases your account, your published ratings and your friendships right away. Your own log in your browser is not touched.

Questions

Use the Suggest something box in Playback's Settings to reach the person who runs the app.
